## Architecture decisions

1. **The server owns combat.** The client sends only a unit aim vector. The server checks the session (live character), rate budget, reload state, sprint, ammo and cadence. It chooses the origin (the eye for the server's stance), adds spread with its own RNG, raycasts, and resolves damage. Ammo counts and hit results reach the client only through `WeaponState` and `ShotResult`.
2. **All tuning is data.** Weapons point at a caliber, calibers list their rounds, and targets wear armor by id. Adding content means editing `Data/` only (see below).
3. **The damage rules are pure.** `Combat/Damage.luau` has no requires and no Roblox APIs, so `tests/run.luau` runs it in the plain Luau CLI. It takes the zone rules as a parameter.
4. **Sessions are per life.** `PlayerSessions` creates a fresh session on every spawn. Delayed work (reloads) checks `isCurrent`, so callbacks from a previous life can't touch the new one. Future per-life systems (health, bleeding, inventory-in-raid) attach to the session the same way `weapon` does.
5. **One remote per message, one gate.** `Net.luau` defines every remote and its payload. Every client→server handler goes through `PlayerSessions.gate`: a live character plus a token bucket of 24 burst and 16/s.
6. **Client prediction is cosmetic only.** Recoil, flash and sound play on click. Counts, hits and damage wait for the server.
7. **Recoil can't drift aim.** The camera pitch from recoil is undone before Roblox's camera update each frame and re-applied after it. Follow-up shots climb while recoil is up, then the view returns, with no random shake.
8. **The server owns stance; the client predicts it.** Keys: C crouch, X prone, Space gets up one stance, Shift from crouch/prone stands you up. The client sends `SetMovement(seq, stance, aiming, sprinting)` and immediately lowers its own HipHeight and eases the camera. The server checks the transition time (`Movement` table, prone is slowest) and, when rising, headroom (a spherecast up to the new eye height + clearance). It then sets WalkSpeed (capped at `transitionSpeed` mid-change), HipHeight, jump, and the body pose, and answers `MovementState(seq, stance)`. If the answer to the latest request differs, the client takes the server's stance. Shots can't be fired while getting into or out of prone.
9. **Poses are real hitboxes.** `StancePose` tweens R15 joint C0s on the server, so the parts the server raycasts against are lower when crouched and flat when prone. Crouch bends the legs with `Movement.legAngles` so feet stay planted. Prone tips the body face-down at the waist and slides it back so the head sits over the root part, where the camera and shot origin are. While prone the body parts are massless and in the `LastShiftProneBody` collision group (no world collision); server hitscan rays use `LastShiftHitscan` so they still hit them.
10. **Camera and shot origin agree.** Roblox puts the first-person camera 1.5 studs above the root part. `CameraOffset` makes up the difference to a smoothed eye height (`Stance.cameraBaseline` / `Stance.eyeHeight`), and the server fires from `Stance.eyePosition` for the same stance.

## Combat rules (prototype values)

- **Penetration ≥ protection:** penetrated. Full damage; on the head this is lethal.
- **Head (helmet) with penetration < protection:** stopped (0 damage). Helmets never "reduce".
- **Body armor with penetration < 60% of protection:** stopped.
- **Body armor otherwise:** reduced, to 40% damage.
- **No armor:** head is lethal. Torso ×1, arms ×0.65, legs ×0.55.
- **Falloff:** full damage up to `effectiveRange`, then `falloffPerStud` per stud down to `minDamageScale`.

| Ammo | Damage | Penetration | Eff. range |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.56 FMJ | 36 | 45 | 90 |
| 5.56 AP | 31 | 75 | 120 |
| 5.56 HP | 52 | 20 | 65 |

| Armor | Slot | Protection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light helmet | Head | 35 |
| Heavy helmet | Head | 65 |
| Soft vest | Torso | 55 |
| Plate carrier | Torso | 70 |

These are fictional balance values, not real ballistics.

## Adding content

- **New round:** add it to `Data/Ammo.luau` and append its id to its caliber in `Data/Calibers.luau`.
- **New caliber/weapon:** add the caliber, its rounds, a `Data/Weapons.luau` entry, a sound set in `Data/WeaponSounds.luau`, and a model builder in `Visual/WeaponModels.luau` (it must create the `Muzzle`, `Aim`, `Eject`, `Grip`, `Support` and `MagWell` attachments). List it in `Data/Loadout.luau` to make it switchable. ADS alignment comes from the `Aim` attachment, so no per-weapon offsets need hand-tuning beyond `view.hip` and `view.eyeRelief`.
- **New armor:** add it to `Data/Armor.luau`. Give a target `Helmet` / `BodyArmor` attributes with the armor id (empty string = none).
- **New target:** add it to `tools/gen_targets.py` and re-run it (or put a Model in `Workspace.LastShiftRange.Targets` by hand) with `Health` / `MaxHealth` and armor attributes, plus BasePart children with a `Zone` attribute (`Head`, `Torso`, `Arms`, `Legs`).

Run `build.bat` afterwards. The data-integrity tests catch broken references (an ammo id missing from its caliber, a weapon pointing at an unknown caliber, and so on).

## Known issues / limitations

- Weapon sounds come from Roblox's licensed Pro Sound Effects library. They were picked by description, not by ear: if one plays a double shot or sounds wrong, change its `length`, pitch or id in `Data/WeaponSounds.luau`.
- The third-person gun is welded to the chest and doesn't pitch with where the player looks (the server doesn't know the camera). R15 arms reach it with IKControls; R6 avatars get a static shoulder pose.
- The old four range targets were replaced by the generated test-range layout; they remain in git history (commit 2e6bde7).
- Stance poses are procedural joint offsets on top of Roblox's default animations, R15 only. On R6 avatars stances change speed, camera and shot origin but not the pose. Arms aren't posed yet (they belong with the third-person weapon hold). Real crouch/prone animations can replace `StancePose` later without changing the stance rules.
- While prone the root part is still a 2-stud upright box, so you need about 2.5 studs of clearance to crawl under something, and the trailing legs pass through walls.
- Stance and speed are server-decided, but the owning client simulates its own character physics. Real movement-integrity checks (speed, height, teleport) belong to the multiplayer milestone.
- Players can't damage each other yet. `TargetService` handles range dummies only.
- The loadout is fixed (all four guns). Picking up, dropping and stashing weapons come with the inventory work.
- Weapon names are generic (AR-556, KR-47, PDW-46, HC-50) on purpose: real names such as Desert Eagle or MP7 are trademarks.
